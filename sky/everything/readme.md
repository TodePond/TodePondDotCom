# 

I did two runs at the Edinburgh Fringe. the second time was the most impactful to my life. 

the first time i just had a small slot on someone else's show. we stayed a little bit further out of the city center. i didn't feel like i was in the thick of it. 

the following year, we brought an entire show. around 7 of us all co-directed and co-created the show. i was the loud annoying host of the show.

we didn't have much money so we got the cheapest of everything. we stayed in a hostel in the city centre. we shared it with many other people. we got to know a really lovely and handsome busker. he shared a room with us. i was above him: i was on the top bunk. he was on the bed below me. like i said he was very handsome and dated a lot - brought someone back home most nights. there were 8 beds in the room, usually 10-13 people sleeping in them each night from around 3am-10am.

each morning i blasted out some music to try to get my fellow cast members up. for some reason it was a lot of Rock DJ by Robbie Williams. i was a very annoying person back then, just like right now but even moreso.

then we got up, had lunch, usually something really cheap and horrendous and filling. and then i downed a massive sugary coffee and we started flyering for the next four hours. then at 5:15pm we did our show in a nightclub. then we watched shows and drank and socialised and danced all night, every night, until around 3am.

and we repeated this every day for 30 days. i don't think it was very healthy really but it was great while it lasted. 

---

we used a lot of costume in our show so it took a little while for everyone to get out of it. my costume tended to be the quickest to take off though it depended on the day (the costume of other characters was different each day, depending on the audience...)

anyway, while we waited for everyone to get changed (which sometimes involved removing lots of face paint and body paint), we sat and watched the show that happened after ours, but downstairs in a slightly smaller room. it was a clowning show, a Gaulier clowning show.

at first i couldn't believe what i was seeing. it was complete and utter nonsense! but it was hilarious. they just stood on stage, repeating the same thing again and again and again, but it was absolutely hilarious.

hey i already wrote a blog post about this kind of clowning so i wont go too into it here. but....

i watched this same show every day, day after day, for an entire month. it was part of the daily ritual. i watched all variations and changes they did throughout the month. i saw the good shows and the bad. i saw them flop, i saw them ascend!

we also got to know those clowns very well. We'd often hang out together after their show, grab food, go and watch shows together.. all the while, trading life stories, trading thoughts and learnings on shows and comedy and everything. we went to cabaret shows together, we danced all night, we even argued! 

one thing that struck me was their CONCRETE confidence. they were completely free: on stage and off it. they were purely present, unwaveringly themselves, whoever they were in that moment. 

we heard all about Gaulier... how the school breaks down your confidence and builds you back up... Which doesn't sound good at all! but the evidence was in front of us: it seemed to bloody work!

i remember one guy really well, who quit his job as a geography teacher, went to clown school and then never looked back. 

one of the clowns we hung out with everyday went on to win Britain's Got Talent, believe it or not! Ziggo.

we all ended up performing on each others shows, like cameos. i remember Ziggo lifting up all our prop furniture during a cameo. "He does that when he's nervous", his clown collaborator told me.

"He does what?"

"He starts weight-lifting."

---

Performing is quite nerve-wracking. The Ed fringe crowd is pretty brutal. if they don't like your shoe they'll just leave. most people are only in town for a few days so every minute counts for them. you're competing against the whole city, which just happens to contain the best performers in the entire world. it's pretty daunting.

some of our shows were massive successes: times when the audience was loving it, laughing along the whole time, getting involved. and also, some of our shows were massive flops. complete failures. the audience just didn't ever get it or click. when the mood in the room is flopped you can't really unflop it, it's gone, and it's painful, excruciatingly painful. fingers get pointed when this happens. with all our tiredness and stress, we'd argue in a mini group. also there was no director to call the shots - we were all in it together doing it collaboratively, so it was sometimes hard to resolve disagreements over direction. Still, we worked it all out and by the end of the month we got something we were really really proud of. funnily enough, we got onto a list of recommendations: "Things to take your kids to". Hooray! (we thoroughly removed all of the swearing, though maybe that's what they liked?)

I almost feel like the flop shows often happened a day after the huge successes. we got cocky and lazy at those times..

the hardest performance was the *final day*, believe it or not. because: by this point the word had gotten out that it was a place to take kids. but... just by chance, a group of loud drunk rugby lads walked in at the last minute. and... although i felt comfortable dealing with hecklers (it's a very heckler friendly show), i wasn't so sure how to do it at the same time as having a bunch of young kids in the audience, especially when a lot of the drunk lad heckles were *quite not-child-friendly*. anyway, it was a flop. what a way to go! i can only look back and laugh and learn. 

what i take from all the flopping is... fearlessness. I'm not afraid of public speaking or anything like that purely because I've experienced the absolute worst case scenarios many times over. i'm not sure there's any easy way round it. i learned that the best way to get good at something is by failing spectacularly repeatedly, until you somehow ascend through it.

when i pick up a microphone, or i walk on stage, i know people will listen, i know how to get people to listen and stop looking at their phone or walking out because i have *failed* to do it hundreds of times, and 60 of those times were with the hardest clientele in the world: Ed fringe boozers and kids. 

---

BUT there's one thing i found even more nerve-wracking than the performance itself: 

i always got really really really nervous about how many people would turn up. the sight of empty chairs filled me with fear. if no one comes... and we've got an empty house..... that would be so cringe + embarrassing + humiliating, right?

if there's just one or two audience members, that would be even worse. because if there's *no* audience you could just cancel and no one would see. but if there's only a small handful of a crowd...... then it would be awful... as performers we'd *outnumber* them. how embarrassing!!!!

well of course I'm foreshadowing that this did eventually happen. it wasn't for our own show: we always did a pretty good job at flyering because we had a big gimmick, which helped a lot. but there was basically an empty show slot in our venue because one of the other shows got cancelled for some controversial / drama reason (there was gossip about it but i didn't know what the truth was). anyway, the rest of the shows in that venue went on a rota to fill that slot so that the organisers could fulfill their promise to the venue and so that any audience members with outdated programmes wouldn't get disappointed when they turn up. 

when it was our turn, we agreed to do a collab with the clowns from downstairs. we planned to do a kind of mashup cabaret show with them. 

we didn't promote the show that much, so i should have seen this coming, but yeah it was like 4 people in the crowd only, and like 6 of us performers. i was starting to panic, i started to get really anxious. i was wondering if we'd have to cancel, i was asking if we should cancel it panic panic

then two of the clowns we were performing with came up the stairs, ready to start, with beaming smiles on their face. they looked really really excited. "OKAY LET'S DO THIS" "THIS IS GONNA BE GREAT" "I'M REALLY LOOKING FORWARD TO THIS" "LET'S GIVE THEM THE SHOW OF THEIR LIFE" "LET'S GO FOR IT" "HOLD NOTHING BACK" and so on. and i gotta tell ya, it put my heart at complete ease. wow. to see such ..... something or other in the face of this situation, which i had considered an absolute nightmare worst-case-scenario not too long ago...

it's like something clicked there and then for me. yes we smashed the show. the audience absolutely loved it. they gave huge donations at the end! and we all celebrated together, changed together. 

after that point, when i found myself in some tricky or nerve-wracking situations... what i took forward is that I'll always try my very best and give it my all. in performances I'll give you my everything, no matter the occasion. I'll hold nothing back. whether i'm performing at a gigantic venue (like the 1200-sized audience i spoke in front of this year) or a miniscule one (i played music to 4 people in a local bar) i will give it my fucking everything.

regrets only come from holding back, forcing yourself to fit some kind of— don't guess what people want or what you need to do. it's best to be yourself and FUCKING GO FOR IT.

---

this fearlessness I gained has taken me to many places. it has gotten me into all sorts of trouble too.

i think i feel most free and at home when I'm living that ed fringe lifestyle, wake up, power up, promote my art, do my art, give everything, leave nothing behind, then immerse myself in the art of the friends around me, dancing together all night.

i don't think it's a sustainable way to live, but it's what i need at times: raving til sunrise in Tivissa, jamming outside the algorave toilet, doing an all-nighter at the algoriver.

i hunt down like minded people wherever i travel. in some places i find them, in some places i don't. I'm pleased ive found so many though. I'm rich with the friends in my wallet! I'm even richer if you count all the people who disapprove of my naivety, or something or other they don't like about me. i know freedom when i see it / feel it. i had it back then, I've got it now. through my many careers and distractions, I'll keep coming back to the lessons i learned back then.
